Africa’s single market clears final hurdle as ten nations ratify digital-trade protocol

The breakthrough unlocks tariff-free data flows across a 1.4-billion-person market and hands small exporters a direct line to continental buyers.

By Aline Uwase
Senior Business Correspondent · Jul 6, 2026 · 6 min read
SHARE

KIGALI — After three years of stop-start negotiations, the African Continental Free Trade Area reached a milestone on Tuesday as a tenth member state ratified its long-delayed digital-trade protocol, the threshold required to bring the rules into force across the bloc.

The protocol governs how data, digital services and e-commerce move between the 54 signatory economies. Negotiators say it removes the single biggest barrier facing small exporters: the patchwork of national rules that made selling across borders slow and expensive.

What changes for exporters

Under the new framework, a cooperative in Musanze selling processed coffee to a buyer in Accra will no longer need to register separately in each market. Digital customs documents recognised in one country will be honoured across the bloc.

Analysts caution that implementation will be uneven. Several economies still lack the digital-ID and payments infrastructure the protocol assumes, and enforcement will depend on a dispute-settlement body that remains understaffed.

The road to full rollout

A phased timeline gives member states until 2028 to align domestic law. In the interim, a continental sandbox will let startups test cross-border products under a single provisional licence — a measure Kigali lobbied hard to include.

For now, the mood among delegates was cautiously celebratory. “We have argued about this room by room,” one negotiator said. “Today we finally agreed on the building.”

CORRECTION

Jul 6, 2026, 14:20 — An earlier version stated nine nations had ratified the protocol. The correct figure is ten.
